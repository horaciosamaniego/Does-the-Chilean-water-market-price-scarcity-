# Does the Chilean water market price scarcity?

Data and analysis code for *Drought, spillovers and water rights transactions in Mediterranean Chile*.

### Abstract
```text
Chile’s 1981 Water Code established one of the world’s most thoroughly market-based systems of water allocation, on the premise that tradable rights would price scarcity and move water toward its highest-value use. We examine whether prices in that market carry a hydrological signal, using 8,020 water rights transactions recorded in fifteen river basins of Mediterranean Chile between 2005 and 2014. A dynamic spatial Durbin model, selected against simpler spatial specifications by likelihood ratio, relates monthly transaction prices to market conditions and to a twelve-month Standardized Precipitation Index constructed from a forty-two year basin climatology.

Prices respond to accumulated precipitation only at the annual scale, and the response reverses sign across basin boundaries. A one-unit improvement in the drought index raises prices by roughly a third where it occurs and lowers them by close to a fifth in neighbouring basins, so that regionally synchronous drying produces a far weaker response than a localised one. Administrative signals dominate hydrological ones. A water scarcity decree in the preceding month raises prices by about half, and mining presence in adjacent basins moves prices some eight times more than mining within the basin itself. Prices transmit across basins with a lag rather than contemporaneously.

Bias-corrected scenario projections imply that equilibrium prices fall by about a quarter by 2100 under SSP5-8.5 and remain unchanged under SSP1-2.6. A market that devalues entitlements as water grows scarcer transmits no incentive to conserve it, a failure the 2022 reform of the Water Code addresses only indirectly.
```

## What this repository contains

Water rights transactions from fifteen river basins of Mediterranean Chile, 2005–2014, together with the catchment precipitation record used to build the drought index, and the code that estimates a dynamic spatial Durbin model of transaction prices and projects them under two SSP scenarios.

The headline results are that prices respond to accumulated precipitation only at the twelve-month scale, that the response is positive in the own basin and negative in neighbouring basins, and that an administrative water scarcity decree moves prices several times more than any hydrological variable.

## Reproducing the results

```bash
git clone https://github.com/horaciosamaniego/chile-water-rights-scarcity.git
cd chile-water-rights-scarcity
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_all.py          # or: make all
```

This writes `output/model.txt`, `output/projection.txt` and regenerates `figures/figure_projection.pdf`. Total runtime is under two minutes on a laptop. The figure is deterministic: the Monte Carlo draws are seeded, so a correct run reproduces the published figure exactly.

## Layout

```
data/     input files, unmodified
src/      analysis code
output/   console results, regenerated by run_all.py
figures/  Figure 2 of the paper, regenerated by src/make_figure.py
docs/     project site (GitHub Pages)
```

### `src/wr_spatial_corrected.py`

Builds the Standardized Precipitation Index from the monthly precipitation record, assembles the panel, constructs the spatial weight matrix, estimates the spatial autoregressive and spatial Durbin specifications, tests one against the other by likelihood ratio, and reports the effects decomposition.

### `src/wr_projection.py`

Computes the projected drought index under SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5 with a delta-change bias correction, solves the long-run equilibrium of the dynamic spatial Durbin model, and propagates coefficient uncertainty by Monte Carlo.

### `src/make_figure.py`

Draws Figure 2. Imports from the two modules above, so run it from the repository root.

Files use `;` as field separator and `,` as decimal mark. Basin identifiers are DGA catchment codes. Prices are logarithms of mean transaction price in Unidades de Fomento.

`pp_total_anual.csv` is retained because it is the file referenced in earlier drafts. It is a strict subset of `pp_historica_mensual.csv` and is not used by the analysis.

Basins 43 and 55 appear in the transaction panel but have no precipitation record, so the analysis drops them and runs on fifteen basins.

## Notes on the drought index

The index follows McKee et al. (1993). Precipitation is accumulated over a window of *k* months, a two-parameter gamma is fitted to the strictly positive accumulations of each calendar month separately, and Thom's mixed-distribution correction is applied so that months with no precipitation are handled rather than discarded. Fitting per calendar month is necessary in a strongly seasonal climate, and the zero correction is necessary because up to a fifth of Decembers in these basins record no precipitation.

Three windows are computed and all three are reported, because the accumulation scale at which the market responds is itself a result.

## Notes on the spatial weight matrix

The weight matrix is defined once at basin level from the four nearest neighbouring centroids, row-standardised, and then blocked across periods. Building it from a stacked panel instead would place every observation at distance zero from other months of the same basin, since coordinates repeat, and the resulting matrix would encode within-basin serial correlation rather than spatial adjacency.
